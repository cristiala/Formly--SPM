Aggregate findings

Across our market research of existing fitness alternatives, several distinct patterns emerged regarding how people approach working out independently.

We found that users value immediate feedback over post-workout analysis, as beginners and casual gym-goers frequently struggle with form but cannot consistently afford or access a personal trainer. Furthermore, research into comparable apps and tutorials showed that pre-recorded coaching videos and extensive tutorials often cause user fatigue because they feel redundant and require passive learning, leaving users wanting active guidance during their movement rather than having to pause and watch instructional clips.

Across our target audience segments, recurring patterns showed that users are willing to grant necessary camera permissions and use functional personal devices if it means minimizing injury risk, saving money on personal training, and boosting their overall gym confidence.

Ultimately, users do not need more passive instructional content. They need real-time, corrective feedback that acts as a virtual spotter to bridge the gap between working out alone and hiring an expensive personal trainer.

Top pick: deep dive

The single strongest finding our team landed on during our market and user research is that users demand real-time, active form correction over post-workout analysis or passive instructional videos. This insight was important because it directly addresses the primary point of our target audience: beginners and casual gym-goers who want the guidance and safety of a personal trainer but cannot consistently afford or access one. While exploring alternative tools and existing fitness apps, we discovered a major behavioral pattern where users experience high drop-off rates from pre-recorded videos because stopping to watch instructional clips interrupts their workout flow and causes cognitive fatigue.

The evidence backing this finding comes directly from our comparative analysis of existing fitness apps and user feedback segments, which revealed that users are willing to grant necessary camera permissions and use their personal devices if an application can actively monitor their movements and provide instant feedback. This minimizes injury risk and boosts gym confidence precisely when it matters most.
