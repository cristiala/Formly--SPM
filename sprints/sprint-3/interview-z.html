Interview Z

Competitive Landscape

Nike Training Club, as documented in Viviana's and Cristina's hands-on teardown on Android and compared with Participant Z's experience.

ProductStrengthGap observed in this test
Nike Training ClubFree, available on Android, and organized into structured, timed workouts. Short drill videos (about 9 to 24 seconds) are available for exercises within a workout.Searching "split squat" did not surface a split squat demonstration. Videos were hidden behind "View drills," showed only one camera angle, and gave no feedback on the user's own form.

Teardown findings:

  • Search: Typing "split squat" into the search bar returned full workouts, such as 60-Min Full Court Strength & Power, 45-Min Power for Runners, and 45-Min Strength & Stability for Runners, rather than anything visibly labeled split squat.
  • Workout page: Opening 60-Min Full Court Strength & Power showed a Start Workout button and a list of timed exercises (for example, 30 seconds of deadbug floor slides, a 30-second hip lift hold, 20 seconds of isometric split squats on each side, and 20 seconds of pogo jumps), with no videos on that screen. Unfamiliar exercise names were not explained there.
  • Drill videos: Demonstration videos were only available under "View drills" and ranged from about 9 to 24 seconds.
  • Camera framing: Videos used a three-quarter or side view. Some demonstrators were not centered, and in the pogo jump video the demonstrator was far from the camera. Most demonstrators were men.
  • Own form: With only one angle per video, the reviewer could not see the movement from other sides and could not tell whether their own position was correct. The app gave no feedback on the user's movement.

Connection to Interview Z: The teardown matches Participant Z's experience. The app is useful for organizing workouts, but finding a specific exercise took extra steps, and even after watching the demonstration the reviewer, like Z, was left unsure whether their form was right.

Note on device limitations: Several apps that offer camera-based form feedback, such as Form Coach and Gymscore, are currently available only on Apple devices. Because the reviewer uses an Android phone and a non-Apple laptop, these apps could not be tested, and a browser-based alternative was unavailable at the time of review. Nike Training Club was selected instead because it is available on Android and is the app Participant Z uses.

VC Doc

Change note

What changed: A third participant, using a different app from Interviews X and Y, described the same gap: demonstrations show how a movement should look but do not tell the user whether their own form is right.

Why: Participant Z rewatched the split squat demonstration a couple of times and still was not sure about their technique. Without being prompted, they described wanting an app that analyzes form through the phone's camera.

Impact: This strengthens the case for testing camera-based form feedback, and positions Formly as a lower-cost alternative to a personal trainer. A next test should check what price participants consider "affordable."

Candidate risk entries

  • Switching from an established app: Participant Z is happy with how Nike Training Club organizes workouts and keeps them consistent, so they may not want to replace it. Suggested owner: Business lead. Trigger to watch: Interviewees say they would not switch from their current app. Possible mitigation: Test Formly as a companion that adds form feedback alongside existing workout apps.
  • Price sensitivity: The participant has spent little on improving form and specifically wants an affordable option. Suggested owner: Business lead. Trigger to watch: Interviewees reject the proposed price as too high. Possible mitigation: Test lower price tiers or a free trial against the cost of personal training.
  • Feedback accuracy and safety: Users who rely on an app instead of a trainer could learn incorrect form if the feedback is wrong. Suggested owner: Technical lead. Trigger to watch: App cues disagree with a trainer's review during testing. Possible mitigation: Validate cues against trainer reviews and keep feedback conservative when tracking confidence is low.

Field notes

Participant Z (team interview)

  • Behavior: Uses Nike Training Club about two to three hours a week to follow workout plans and review exercise demonstrations. During a recent squat workout, opened the app, searched for the split squat, watched the demonstration a couple of times, and tried the exercise again while following the instructions, but was still unsure whether the form was correct. Started using the app to get in shape and follow a structured routine, and still uses it to stay consistent. Has not spent much money on improving form. Has a friend who works with a personal trainer.
  • Opinion: Finds the demonstrations clear and helpful for learning new exercises, but feels the app lacks personalized feedback and the instructions are sometimes not detailed enough. Wished the app could analyze their movements and point out mistakes. Would prefer one app that combines exercise guidance with form feedback, and would try an affordable app that analyzes form through the phone camera. Believes trainers give more individualized guidance and that their friend seems more confident because of the immediate feedback.

Contradiction memo

Key contradiction: Participant Z says Nike Training Club's clear instructions make them more confident trying new exercises, yet after rewatching a demonstration they still could not tell whether their own form was correct. They also value the individualized feedback a trainer gives but do not want to pay for regular training sessions.

Recommendation: Test whether camera-based feedback closes the gap that demonstrations leave, and test pricing that sits clearly below the cost of a personal trainer.
