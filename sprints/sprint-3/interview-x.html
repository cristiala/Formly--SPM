Field notes

X Interview

Formly team interview with anonymized participant X. Answers are paraphrased from the interview notes.

Behavior tags describe X's account of specific events and actions, including his immediate response. Opinion tags describe broader judgments, preferences, or possibilities. Both come from X's account, not direct observation.

1. Screen 1 minute

Interviewer: What do you currently use to help you check or improve your exercise form?

X · Behavior: He reported using the free Hevy app for a routine and watching the short video clip provided for each exercise.

2. Last time story 4 minutes

Interviewer: Can you walk me through the last time you checked whether you were doing an exercise correctly?

X · Behavior: Earlier that week, Hevy gave him a routine with Romanian deadlifts, which he had done only a few times. He watched the clip of a person bending over while keeping their back straight and tried to copy it. He felt the effort in his lower back instead of his hamstrings, rewatched the clip about three times, and still could not tell what he was doing wrong. He decided to look for an alternative exercise in a future session.

3. Alternatives 3 minutes

Interviewer: What other methods have you tried to check your form, and why did you stop using them?

X · Behavior: He tried recording himself on his phone, once asked another gym-goer to check his form, and now relies on the app's videos.

X · Opinion: He said propping his phone on a water bottle and watching the recording in the gym felt inconvenient. He did not want to take much of the other person's time. The app's videos felt easier to use, though they did not always help him feel that he was doing the exercise correctly.

4. Switching story 4 minutes

Interviewer: What made you start using the Hevy app? Are you still using it? If not, why?

X · Behavior: He started using Hevy, still uses its plans, and usually watches the video when the app suggests an unfamiliar variation.

X · Opinion: He wanted a plan because he did not know what to do at the gym. He can see the movement in the video but, depending on the exercise, cannot tell whether his own movement is correct.

5. Money and time 2 minutes

Interviewer: How much time or money have you actually spent on checking or improving your form recently?

X · Behavior: He reported spending $0 on form checking and rewatching the roughly 10-second clips at the gym. He estimated that trying to work out the movement adds about 10-15 minutes to his workout.

6. Close 1 minute

Interviewer: Is there someone you know who has tried a similar method or something different?

X · Opinion: He has considered trying a trainer after people he knows who have used one recommended it.

Hevy teardown

EP Doc

Edward Puentes · Teardown #1

Interview context

After interviewing one gym-goer, X, I chose Hevy for a hands-on teardown. X used its free plans and videos but could not correct a Romanian deadlift after watching the clip three times; recording himself in the gym felt inconvenient.

Starting an exercise

Hevy offers a free exercise library. From the main page, I could select an exercise and start it. I could watch a video when I wanted a demonstration. The path to active guidance was harder to understand: I did not find a clear “guide me” action from the main page. The UI looked polished, but the navigation was not always intuitive for someone seeking form help.

Logging and free use

Workout logging worked well. The app made it easy to record the workout and return to the routine, and the videos were useful when I was confused about a movement. The free version did not load me with ads. These are real strengths; Formly should complement them rather than assume users need a new workout log.

Pricing and Pro features

The listed Hevy Pro price was $2.99 per month or $23.99 per year in USD. Personalized programs and Trainer guidance are Pro features. Pro also offers more extensive progress reporting, while the free version still supports core logging and some progress viewing. Hevy's pricing comparison lists the same prices, and its Pro feature table describes the feature limits.

Points of hesitation

  • Personalized sets: I could log sets in the free version, but it did not suggest sets, weights, or reps tailored to me. Those recommendations are part of Hevy Trainer in Pro.
  • Feedback: The personalized training feedback I found was reserved for Pro. I did not find live correction of my exercise form.
  • Video variety: The free exercise library showed one demonstration video per exercise, with no second view to try when a clip was unclear.

Strengths, gaps, and Formly implication

Strengths: Hevy has a useful free exercise library, good workout logging, helpful demonstration clips, an attractive interface, and an ad-free free experience.

Gaps: The path to form guidance is not obvious from the main page, and a demonstration does not assess the user's own movement in real time. X's interview shows the practical consequence: repeated viewing did not tell him what to change.

Formly implication: Test whether timely feedback about the user's own form resolves that gap without adding the phone setup burden X described. Audio or haptic cues are possibilities to test, not findings from this teardown.

Contradiction memo

Key contradiction: An exercise video does not always help a user understand whether their own movement is correct. X rewatched one several times and still could not identify what to change.

Recommendation: Test feedback on the user's movement while keeping setup simple enough for use during a workout.
