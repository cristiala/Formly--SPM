Competitive Landscape

The team compared Hevy's workout logging and exercise videos with Formly's proposed form feedback. The interview and teardown show why seeing a demonstration may still leave a user unsure about their own movement.

Product Strength Gap
Hevy Useful free library, smooth workout logging, videos, polished UI, and no ads in the free experience No live form correction; the path to guidance was not intuitive

Field notes

Participant X (team interview)

  • Behavior: X rewatched a Romanian deadlift video about three times, felt the effort in his lower back rather than his hamstrings, and still could not tell what to change. He tried recording himself, once asked another gym-goer for help, reported spending $0 on form help, and estimated that confusion added 10-15 minutes to his workout.
  • Opinion: He found phone setup inconvenient and has considered trying a trainer.

Contradiction memo

Key contradiction: An exercise video does not always help a user understand whether their own movement is correct. X rewatched one several times and still could not identify what to change.

Recommendation: Test feedback on the user's movement while keeping setup simple enough for use during a workout.
