Competitive Landscape

The team compared Hevy, Life Fitness QR tooling, and Nike Training Club with Formly's proposed form feedback. Across the interviews and teardowns, the recurring pattern is that demonstrations can be useful but still leave users unsure about their own form.

Ranked switching triggers

An evidence-based ranking of what would make people leave their current workout solution for another one.

Rank Trigger Why it matters
1 Reliable form feedback during a workout The strongest trigger is the desire to know whether their movement is correct while they are exercising. Interview X, Interview Y, and Interview Z all described uncertainty after watching demonstrations, and Z explicitly said they would try an affordable app that analyzes form through the phone camera.
2 Lower cost than a trainer without losing personalization Users increasingly prefer a cheaper alternative to paying for a trainer. Interview Y spent around $800 on personal training before deciding to work out independently, and Interview Z said they would choose an affordable camera-based app instead of paying for a trainer.
3 Reduced friction and easier setup A tool that is quick to access and easy to use is more likely to be adopted. Interview Y described relying on mirrors and QR code guides, while the Life Fitness teardown revealed setup friction and prompt interruptions that reduced usefulness.
4 Clearer guidance than video-only content Users do not switch away from a current solution just because a video exists; they switch when the guidance helps them correct their movement. Hevy and Nike Training Club both offered videos, but the teardowns showed they still did not give live feedback on the user’s real form.
5 Better search and exercise discoverability When a workout app makes it difficult to find a specific movement, users may look for alternatives. The Nike Training Club teardown showed that searching for a split squat did not surface a direct result and required extra steps to find the relevant demo.

Overall conclusion: The most compelling switch trigger is not a better library of videos, but an app that can tell users whether their form is correct in real time, at a price they are willing to pay.

Team Change Note

  • Since Sprint 2: The team completed Interviews X, Y, and Z and added supporting research, including Edward's Hevy teardown, Abigail's Life Fitness gym machine QR-code teardown, and Viviana's and Cristina's Nike Training Club teardown.
  • Why: The findings highlighted limitations in existing exercise guidance tools. Users may still struggle to evaluate and correct their own form, even when instructional videos or demonstrations are available. The research also showed that people who work out independently may still benefit from additional exercise guidance.
  • Affected: The Sprint 3 evidence log, competitive landscape, research summary, contradiction memos, candidate risks, and competitor teardown documents.

Candidate risk entries

Draft candidates for team review. Existing risk entries are preserved below.

  • Pose detection: Lighting or camera angle may reduce accuracy. Suggested owner: Technical lead. Trigger to watch: Repeated tracking failures in testing. Possible mitigation: Test calibration prompts and retry guidance.
  • Setup friction: Users may abandon a form check if positioning a phone interrupts their workout. Suggested owner: Product lead. Trigger to watch: Users skip setup in a gym trial. Possible mitigation: Test fewer setup steps and hands-free guidance.
  • Willingness to pay: A capable free workout tracker may make a second subscription hard to justify. Suggested owner: Business lead. Trigger to watch: Future interviewees reject the proposed price. Possible mitigation: Test pricing and Formly's value as a companion to existing trackers.
